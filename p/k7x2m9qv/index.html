But I don't see AI experiences and trust as a separate thing. It's actually the clear path into the future of this same work.

In my MRV work, I designed interfaces where every number could be traced back to its source and verified. That's exactly the kind of trust problem AI features raise. So I'd say both, together.

We had a suite of apps for organizations in ESG and compliance, and every client worked completely differently: different names for the same process, different spreadsheets, different visualizations. There was no shared standard, and every new client meant rebuilding similar flows from scratch.

At first I took each case individually and designed a workflow for it. Then I realized that underneath the mess, the journey was always the same: assess, make a plan, implement improvements, and track data long term, so clients could make better decisions and present to investors. I mapped that shared pattern and designed a single framework for the whole suite.

The trade-off was standardization versus customization. Too rigid and clients wouldn't adopt it, too flexible and we'd lose the efficiency. So I locked the core workflow and left flexibility only at the edges, like how each client phrased their questions.

We could present it to every new client as a ready solution, and we scaled without reworking the same workflow over and over.
