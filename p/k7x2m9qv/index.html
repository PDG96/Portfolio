I took two teams doing the same job in completely different ways and turned it into one workflow.

A client operating in Peru and Colombia was digitalizing their process. Both teams assessed mining sites, created improvement plans and tracked implementation, but with different approaches and terminology, compiling information by hand into spreadsheets and comparing manually.

Instead of designing two interfaces, I mapped everything that correlated and defined one shared nomenclature. The trade-off was standardization versus each team's own way of working. I locked the core flow and kept flexibility only where their local context really needed it.

Result: both countries on one system, no more manual spreadsheet comparison, and a framework we reused for new clients without reworking the same flow.

I didn't force adoption, I designed for it. I added a comment field on every question in the demo, so users could flag anything they weren't sure about.

Showing the design to a manager is very different from users working in it, so we ran rounds of testing and fine-tuning. I turned their comments into informational text and onboarding guidance, so they felt at ease with the new workflow.

Result: both teams adopted the same system, and the questions dropped from one round to the next.

For me, AI in a product like Workiva is a trust problem before it's an interface problem. An auditor has to be able to defend every number, including the ones an agent produced.
