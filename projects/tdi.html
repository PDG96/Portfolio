The Challenge

Due diligence was static, and the world wasn't.

Sourcing commodities like copper, cobalt and gold from high-risk regions demands meticulous due diligence — OECD Annex II, AML/CFT checks, watchlist screening, site audits, ESG scoring. Traditional approaches were static: a supplier filled in a form, an analyst scored it, and six months later the risk profile was already out of date.

Compliance teams juggled disconnected tools for financial verification, watchlist cross-referencing, audit tracking and ESG reporting — with no way to triangulate evidence across sources or surface red flags automatically.

The Solution

Living supplier profiles, always up to date.

An integrated platform where each supplier is a continuously evolving record. Inherent risk, residual risk and audit performance are tracked as three independent dimensions. Triangulation from multiple sources — self-assessment, third-party audits, independent research — keeps every field current and traceable.

Red flags surface automatically when new information contradicts a supplier's record. Reports are generated on demand for any regulatory framework — OECD, LBMA, RMI — with full source-tracking baked in.

Research & Discovery

Inside the compliance officer's day.

I spent weeks embedded with compliance analysts, supply chain managers and third-party auditors — the people who actually run due diligence. What I learned broke my initial assumptions: the work wasn't "filling in forms", it was detective work. Analysts spent most of their time cross-referencing public filings, news reports, UN Group of Experts updates, leaked documents and informal networks. The platform had to support that investigation, not replace it.

The biggest insight: a due diligence report is the output of the work, not the work itself. TDI had to make the continuous, evidence-gathering work visible, structured, and shareable.

Key Insights

What the analysts taught me.

01

Due diligence is a verb, not a document.

A report is the output, not the work. Analysts spend most of their time gathering and cross-referencing evidence. The platform had to support that ongoing investigation — not treat the final PDF as the goal.

02

One risk score hides more than it reveals.

Users needed to see inherent risk, residual risk, and audit performance as independent dimensions. A supplier might be in a high-risk country but have excellent internal controls — collapsing that into a single number would mislead.

03

Every data field has a source — and the source matters.

Self-reported data must be weighed differently from third-party audits, which must be weighed differently from independent research. Source-tracking had to be a first-class concept on every field, not an afterthought buried in metadata.

04

Watchlists alone are never enough.

Compliance teams cross-reference international watchlists, UN reports, litigation databases, news outlets and local media. The platform had to make all of these feel like a single unified view — not six browser tabs.

Design Principles

Four rules that guided every decision.

P1

Living profiles

Every supplier is a continuously evolving record. The last update timestamp is never hidden.

P2

Source-aware data

Every field carries its provenance. Users always know where a piece of information came from.

P3

Progressive disclosure

Scores first, evidence behind. Users drill into complexity only when the score alone isn't enough.

P4

Cross-reference by default

No single source is trusted alone. The platform triangulates before presenting a finding.

Explorations

How to show risk without oversimplifying it.

The hardest design problem in TDI was showing complex, multi-dimensional risk without reducing it to a single score. I explored three approaches before landing on a design that gives users a quick read and preserves nuance.

Concept A — Traffic Light

A · Abandoned

Single composite score

A traffic-light score hiding all complexity. Quick to scan, but analysts said it was dangerous: the score felt authoritative in ways the underlying data didn't support. Tempted to trust it even when it was wrong.
